# Visual cadence and static mockup gates

Use this process when a reel includes proof cards, explanatory animations, diagrams, or a new motion family.

## Script-role classification

Before assigning visuals, mark each spoken section as one of:

- **Hook or connection:** keep the speaker visible with captions unless real opening proof is stronger.
- **Proof:** use a real screenshot, product recording, event asset, official source, or supplied evidence.
- **Explanation:** use a diagram or restrained animation only when the idea is still abstract.
- **Breather:** use clean A-roll and captions so the viewer can reconnect with the speaker.
- **Payoff:** use the strongest A-roll, proof, or restrained emphasis treatment.

Do not treat every sentence as an animation opportunity. A visual must clarify, prove, mark a structural beat, or restore attention after a dense section.

## Static mockup gate

Before creating an expensive or generative animation:

1. Build one representative still at the actual output dimensions.
2. Inspect it at phone scale.
3. Check claim accuracy, brand match, typography, hierarchy, safe zones, speaker visibility, and proof readability.
4. Reject generic AI imagery, synthetic stock-looking scenes, invented proof, inconsistent characters, or burned text that will be hard to revise.
5. Approve the still before creating motion.

A successful render is not design approval.

## Reference priority

Use references in this order:

1. Prior approved series masters.
2. User-supplied assets and examples.
3. Official brand materials and real proof.
4. Carefully selected external inspiration.

A random inspiration board must not override established series identity.

## Train with reasons

When learning a new edit behavior, save:

- the accepted example;
- why the hook, cut, proof, or motion was chosen;
- what was removed;
- which alternatives were rejected and why;
- the series boundary for the learned rule.

A final video without editorial rationale is weak training data.

## Layer separation

Keep these separately editable until final assembly:

- A-roll and selected dialogue;
- captions;
- proof or B-roll;
- generated or coded motion;
- SFX;
- music;
- timeline and visibility state.

Do not accept AI animations with baked music or uncontrollable SFX as a default production asset.

## Timeline plan

Every non-A-roll visual should record:

- exact start and end;
- spoken phrase covered;
- role: proof, explanation, emphasis, or transition;
- source file and reuse-rights status;
- editorial reason;
- speaker visibility state;
- caption state;
- approval status of the static mockup.

Reject a visual whose role or reason is vague.

## Series boundaries

- Shivank: real, claim-specific proof remains the default. Generic AI image cards are not B-roll.
- YC/Y-series: keep the speaker unobstructed and preserve the official white/orange Arial system.
- Selvam/Mindwise: use the separate authority/trust style. Dense AI cards and constant animation are not transferable.
